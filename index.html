How to play

This is a spoken storytelling game for two or more players.

Purpose: Practice explaining your reasoning and reconsidering a situation as new information appears.

  1. One player draws a prompt and reads it aloud, or uses the website to read it.
  2. The next player suggests the worst possible response to the situation.
  3. The following player adds information that makes that response seem reasonable.
  4. The next player adds information that makes the same response seem unreasonable again.
  5. Continue taking turns, changing whether the response seems reasonable or unreasonable.

Keep the original action the same. Add new information to change how people judge it. You may add information, but you cannot contradict facts already established in the story.

The round ends when a player gives up or cannot provide a valid continuation. The last player to give a complete valid answer earns a point. Players decide together whether an answer follows the rules.

With two players, simply alternate turns.

You may ask to hear the prompt again or ask another player to recap the story at any time, without penalty.

Example

Prompt: You wake up and your phone is dead.

Bad response: Throw the phone away.

New information that supports the response: The phone is broken beyond repair.

New information that challenges the response: The broken phone contains a memory card with important photos.

Saying “It only needed charging” would contradict the established fact that the phone was broken beyond repair.
